<Callout type="note" title="SUMMARY">

**Problem.** Front desk staff used a text-only system with nested menus. They also merged handwritten books into it by hand for reports.

**Decision.** I replaced the nested menus with a sidebar that is always visible.

**Result.** The hotel never adopted it. In a usability test with 7 testers, satisfaction averaged 91%.

**Role.** Full-stack developer. I built the front desk module alone.

</Callout>

This was my final project at Politeknik Caltex Riau. A lecturer offered "Furaya Hotel" as a project title, based on a real hotel in Pekanbaru, and I took the front desk module. Other students built other modules separately.

## The problem

In the stakeholder interviews with the hotel's management, the front desk said they used two things at once, a digital system and handwritten books. For reports, including the daily report, staff merged the two by hand. That was slow, and it risked errors.

The digital system was text-only with nested menus. To move to another task, staff pressed back several times to reach the main menu.

## Decisions

### One sidebar instead of nested menus

I never considered another idea for the navigation. The old system is really old, and its UI looks like a command line. To show the menu, it sends you to a whole page. Systems today show the menu in a sidebar, and the content takes most of the screen. That is what I built. Users still see about 80% of the screen as content, and they switch menus in one click. Reservations, check-in and check-out, and room status are one click apart.

### Prototype changes from the hotel's feedback

I built the design in three prototype iterations. The hotel's management reviewed the first two, and their feedback shaped the next iteration each time.

<GraphTimeline title="PROTOTYPES">

- Aug 2024: requirements, feature list, system modeling, first prototype
- Sep 2024: reviewed with the hotel, added a floor plan to each room, simplified check-in and check-out
- Oct to Nov 2024: reviewed again, added online payment and supporting features

</GraphTimeline>

I built the system from March to June 2025. It uses Laravel, Inertia.js, React, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, and MySQL, with Midtrans for payment. I presented it in a final seminar in July 2025.

## Impact

The hotel never used the system, so there are no usage results. I do have a usability test from the final report. Seven respondents answered a 15-question Likert form: 3 in the guest role, 2 staff, and 2 admin. Most were classmates and lecturers, not the hotel's own staff.

<GraphRank title="USABILITY" max={100}>

- 91% satisfaction
- 90% efficiency
- 89% fewer errors
- 87% memorable
- 84% learnability

</GraphRank>

These are average scores from 7 testers. The two highest single items, at 97% each, were recalling the steps quickly after a break and finding it comfortable to use.

## My role

I was the full-stack developer, and the only builder of the front desk module. It covers reservations for staff and the public, check-in and check-out, guests, room status, dashboards, and room management.
