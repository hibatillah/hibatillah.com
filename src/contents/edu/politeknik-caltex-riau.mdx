Our curriculum ran on what everyone called the Packet System. There were no electives and no shortcuts. We went through a fixed list of subjects together, from programming logic to Supply Chain Management to Enterprise Architecture. I came in as a complete beginner who questioned why a `boolean` type even needed to exist. I left as a _Cum Laude_ graduate who could architect a system end to end.

## Business plan competitions

I got into technopreneurship through business plan competitions. Teams pitch a product idea and defend it in front of judges and mentors. One of our entries was [MassBeat](/project/massbeat), a platform to bridge the gap between home workouts and professional trainer consultations. We made it during the transition out of the COVID-19 pandemic.

- We wrote a business plan and high-fidelity mockups.
- We joined the 1000 Startup Digital program, completed its Ideation stage, and advanced to Hacksprint.
- We won 1st Runner Up at GenBI Riau, and 2nd Runner Up at LIKMI #2 and PIKMI, all in 2023.

We had planned to build the full app. But each of us had other things to carry outside MassBeat, so we could not keep up the pace it needed. Together we chose to sunset it. It's still a bit sad. But I got more out of it than I expected. I got real experience shaping a business idea and defending it under pressure. I also saw what it takes to turn a slide into something real.

## Capstone project

Each semester ended with a group project that forced the subjects we had just learned to work together in one running application. The format changed every semester. In one semester it was a capstone, and my team won the _Best Capstone Project Group_ award.

We had to draft a product requirement document and combine three subjects into one application:

- Framework Programming: a web app with `React` and `Express`.
- Supply Chain Systems: the logic for inventory flow and logistics.
- Data Visualization: dashboards for business insights in `Looker Studio`.

## Internship

In my third year I got into MSIB Batch 6, a national internship program that's tough to get into. Through it I became a [Front End Developer](/work/frontend-ministry-finance) at the Ministry of Finance in Jakarta.

It was my first time inside a proper engineering team. I sat with stakeholders and listened to how they ran their day. Then I helped build a web-based room management system for the Ministry's units. That is where the theory I had studied for years met deadlines, standards, and people who depend on what you ship.

## Final project

In my final year, a lecturer offered "Furaya Hotel" as a project title, based on a real hotel in Pekanbaru. I took the front desk module and built it with `Laravel`, `React`, and `Inertia.js`. The hotel never adopted it. The project has [its own page](/project/furaya-hotel-management).

The academic side ran alongside the build. I defended the proposal at the start, tested through development, and gave a final result presentation at the end. So the thesis stopped feeling like a separate paper and felt like the same job, just documented.
